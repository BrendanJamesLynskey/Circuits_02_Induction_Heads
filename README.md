# Circuits 02 — In-context Learning and Induction Heads

**In-context Learning and Induction Heads** (Olsson, Elhage, Nanda, Joseph, DasSarma, Henighan et al., Anthropic, March 2022) makes the case that a small, identifiable circuit — the **induction head** — is the primary mechanism behind **in-context learning** in transformers. An induction head is a two-layer circuit: a **previous-token head** feeds an attention head that does **prefix matching** (attend back to the token that followed the current token's last occurrence) then **copying** (raise that token's logit), completing the pattern `[A][B]…[A] → [B]`. The paper's central finding is the **phase change**: a narrow window early in training where induction heads abruptly form, a bump/kink appears in the loss curve, and the model's **in-context learning score** (loss at the 500th token minus the 50th) jumps sharply. The authors marshal **six converging arguments** — co-occurrence, co-perturbation, and direct ablation (rigorous in small attention-only models) through to continuity (circumstantial for large models), backed by 34 transformers and 50,000+ head ablations. They show the same heads **generalize** from literal copying to fuzzy matching, soft induction, translation and abstract "do the analogous thing" pattern completion. For engineers, it is the mechanistic explanation of **why few-shot prompting and long contexts work**, and a clean example of a capability emerging via a discrete circuit.
